Phase spirals induced by the gas warp

I use N-body SPH simulations of a warped and an unwarped galaxy to search for, and analyze phase space spirals. I find that one-armed phase spirals are widespread in the warped galaxy, and can be sustained by the continuous perturbation of the warp throughout the simulation. These warp-induced phase spirals have consistent characteristics with the Gaia DR3 observations. On the contrary, one-armed phase spirals in the unwarped galaxy are weak and stochastically-distributed.

The figure below illustrates the time evolution of the phase spiral occurrence rate compared with the gas inflow and the bending wave amplitude in the warped simulation. The figure demonstrates that the phase spiral occurrence rate correlates with the gas inflow and the bending wave amplitude, and lags behind the gas inflow by a few hundred Myr.

High resolution chemical abundances of the Nyx stream

I perform high-resolution spectroscopic studies using SMH on 34 stars in the Nyx stream observed with Magellan/MIKE and Keck/HIRES to analyze their chemical abundances. The figure below shows the results of my differential chemical abundance analysis. The figure demonstrates that most of the Nyx stars have consistent chemistry with the metal-rich, high-α thick disk of the Milky Way. However, there are 5 substantially more metal-poor stars ([Fe/H] ∼ −2.0) whose chemical abundances are similar to the metal-weak thick disk.
